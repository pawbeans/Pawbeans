## On-screen lines

Dog Decoded light sourcing (owner, 2026-10-13): popular dog-behaviour lore framed as "can mean" / "often"; no health
claims. The safety lines were checked (read 2026-10-17).

| Line | Basis |
|---|---|
| Sniffing and solving problems is hard work for a dog's brain. | AKC, "Indoor scent games for dogs" (scent games "enrich their minds", keep dogs mentally stimulated indoors), https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/lifestyle/indoor-scent-games-for-dogs/ ; ASPCA, "Canine DIY enrichment" (food puzzles "provide mental stimulation"), https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/dog-care/canine-diy-enrichment |
| 5: the sniff mat. Scatter kibble in it, and they have to slow down and sniff. Sniffing calms them. | ASPCA, "Canine DIY enrichment": snuffle mat (kibble hidden in fleece strips); sniffing can be "stimulating and calming"; food puzzles slow eating. |
| 4: the toilet roll. Drop in a few treats and fold the ends in. They have to work it open. | ASPCA, "Canine DIY enrichment" (paper roll folded closed at both ends with treats inside); AKC, "Do-it-yourself cognitive dog toys for home", https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/lifestyle/do-it-yourself-cognitive-dog-toys-for-home/ |
| 3: the towel burrito. Lay treats on a towel and roll it up. They unroll it, one treat at a time. | AKC, "Do-it-yourself cognitive dog toys for home" (tea towel folded over treats). |
| 2: the lick mat. Spread something on a lick mat, or stuff a rubber toy and freeze it. Licking is soothing, and it takes ages. | Oregon Humane Society, "Using Kong toys" (licking and chewing are soothing; stuff and freeze overnight to slow it down), https://www.oregonhumane.org/training/using-kong-toys/ |
| Stay close for the cardboard and towel games, and take them away if they start chewing them up. | ASPCA, "Canine DIY enrichment": "always closely and actively supervising your dog(s) while DIY items are accessible to them"; if your dog tries to swallow an item, take it away. |
| And count the treats in their food for the day. | WSAVA, "Feeding treats to your dog" (treats no more than 10 % of daily calories), https://wsava.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Feeding-treats-to-your-dog-v2.pdf |
| 1: find it. Ask them to wait, and hide a treat. Then say, find it! Start easy, then try the next room. | ASPCA, "Canine DIY enrichment" ("Find It": start easy, increase the difficulty over time); AKC indoor scent games (box search, change the hiding place each time). |
| Add a little tug and a few minutes of training, and that's a full day's work. A tired dog is a happy dog. | AKC, "Great indoor games to play with your dog", https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/lifestyle/great-indoor-games-to-play-with-your-dog/ ; the payoff is a common saying (lore). |

Cut at build: the pitch's "Ten minutes of sniffing can tire them out like a walk" (no reputable source for the number).
