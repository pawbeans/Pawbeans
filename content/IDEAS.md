## Dog Decoded (viral, no BEANS mention)

**D1 · Where your dog sleeps says a lot about your relationship** ★ owner's example · MADE 2026-10-13 (six spots, `films/2026-10-13-where-your-dog-sleeps`)
- Hook: "Where your dog sleeps says a lot about you." Counter 1/4 → 4/4.
- Beats: on your bed (trust, pack warmth) → by the door (on watch) → touching you, back to you (has your back) →
  under the covers / tucked against your legs (the sweetest: security). Each spot = Miya in that spot, the room drawn
  in two lines, an orange label.
- Ending: "Where does yours sleep? Tell us below." Loop to Miya stretching on the bed.
- Sources: *verify* AKC / behaviourist pieces on sleeping positions and co-sleeping (write as "can mean").
- Miya: lie, ★ curled "donut", ★ back-to-back lean. 25–30 s.

**D2 · Your dog tilts their head because of you**
- Hook: "Why does your dog do this?" (head tilt, freeze frame).
- Beats: tilting happens more to familiar words → the dogs that learn the most toy names tilt the most → it's
  concentration, not confusion → it often goes to the same side every time.
- Ending: "Say their favourite word. Which way do they tilt?" Sources: Sommese et al. 2021, Animal Cognition
  (*verify*). Miya: ★ head tilt. 20–25 s.

**D3 · They evolved puppy eyes for you**
- Hook: "Your dog's eyebrows are a weapon."
- Beats: wolves lack the muscle that lifts the inner eyebrow → dogs have it → it makes the eyes look bigger and sadder →
  people respond to it; it evolved alongside us in a few thousand years.
- Ending: "So yes. They know exactly what they're doing." Source: Kaminski et al. 2019, PNAS (*verify*).
  Miya: ★ close-up brow raise (face option). 20–25 s.

**D4 · The stare that makes you both feel love**
- Hook: "When your dog stares at you, this happens."
- Beats: mutual gaze → oxytocin rises in both of you → the same loop as parent and baby → hard stares mean something
  else (body language contrast).
- Ending: "Go and look at your dog. We'll wait." Source: Nagasawa et al. 2015, Science (*verify*). 20–25 s.

**D5 · Dog years aren't seven**
- Hook: "Your dog is not 7 times your age." Myth/Truth cards.
- Beats: the 1:7 rule is a myth → dogs age fastest in year one (a 1-year-old is roughly a young adult) → it slows after
  → a counter shows Miya's age converting.
- Ending: "How old is yours in human years? Comment and we'll tell you." Source: Wang et al. 2020, Cell Systems
  (epigenetic clock) and AVMA guidance (*verify*). 25–30 s.

**D6 · Why your dog spins before lying down**
- Hook: "Your dog isn't being dramatic." (Miya spinning)
- Beats: inherited nesting: flatten the grass → check for threats → set their scent → find the right temperature.
- Ending: loop: she spins, lies down, and the film starts again. Sources: AKC (*verify*). Miya: spin, lie. 20 s.

**D7 · Chocolate, grapes and the Halloween bowl** (pinned 29 Oct)
- Hook: "Never give your dog these 4 treats." Countdown.
- Beats: chocolate (theobromine, darker = worse) → grapes and raisins → xylitol (sugar-free sweets, gum) →
  macadamias. Calm, not scary. "If it happens: call your vet."
- Ending: "Save this for the sweet bowl." Sources: ASPCA Animal Poison Control, Merck Vet Manual (*verify*). 25–30 s.

**D8 · The Christmas table: what's safe** (pinned 22 Dec)

- Sources: ASPCA, RSPCA (*verify*: cooked bones, onions and garlic, fatty trimmings). 25–30 s.

**D9 · CNY snacks: what your dog can't share** (pinned 3 Feb)

**D10 · The tail wag has a direction**
- Hook: "Watch which way your dog's tail wags."
- Beats: more to the right when they see someone they love → more to the left at something unsettling → other dogs read
  it too. Source: Quaranta et al. 2007, Current Biology; Siniscalchi et al. 2013 (*verify*). Miya: wag (needs a bias
  option). 20–25 s.

**D11 · Your dog can smell time**
- Hook: "Your dog knows when you're coming home. Here's how."
- Beats: your scent fades from the house through the day → they learn how faint it gets by the time you return →
  noses: millions of receptors vs ours. Sources: Horowitz, *Being a Dog* (2016), AKC (*verify* receptor numbers).
  25 s.

**D12 · The "guilty" look isn't guilt**
- Hook: "Your dog doesn't feel guilty. Look again."
- Beats: the look happens when you scold, whether or not they did it → it's appeasement, reading your face → so
  scolding after the fact teaches nothing. Source: Horowitz 2009, Behavioural Processes (*verify*). 25 s.

**D13 · Why they kick the grass after pooping**
- Hook: "It's not tidying up." Beats: scent glands in the paws → a visual and scent signpost. Ending: "Is yours a
  kicker?" Sources: AKC (*verify*). Miya: ★ back-kick. 20 s. (bodily humour, restrained)

**D14 · Dogs poop facing north (sometimes)**
- Hook: "Your dog has a compass."

**B2 · Your treats are ruining the elimination diet**
- Hook: "Your dog's diet trial is failing. Because of one thing."
- Beats: an elimination diet means one new protein for about 8 weeks → one chicken treat resets the clock → treats,
  chews, flavoured meds all count → so the treats must be the same single protein.
- Tie-in: single-ingredient treats in the same novel protein (e.g. Kangaroo). Accent: Kangaroo.
- Sources: Olivry et al. 2015, BMC Veterinary Research (8 weeks) (*verify*). 35–40 s.

**B3 · What "novel protein" actually means**
- Hook: "Chicken isn't a novel protein. Neither is beef."
- Beats: novel = one your dog has never eaten → the immune system has no antibodies to it → that's why kangaroo,
  rabbit, venison, ostrich and horse exist in dog food → "new to your dog", not exotic for the sake of it.
- Tie-in: the six BEANS proteins as six coloured pills. Accent: Rabbit. Sources: WSAVA / vet derm references (*verify*).
  30–35 s.

**B4 · The 4 things dogs are most often allergic to**
- Hook: "Your dog is probably allergic to one of these."

- Source: Mueller, Olivry & Prélaud 2016, BMC Veterinary Research (beef, dairy, chicken, wheat) (*verify*).
  Tie-in: novel proteins. Accent: Venison. 30 s.
