1. ★ Where your dog scratches is a clue (IDEAS B5) · 50–60 s · accent sky (Allergy & Itch)
   Hook: "Here's what your dog's scratching actually means."  ·  Format: map, counter 1/3 → 3/3 (an itch map on Miya)
   Through-line: where they itch, and when in the year, points to what's causing it.
   What it covers:
   - Cold open (cover frame): Miya mid-scratch, scratch ticks flying; hook in Fraunces, "means" in orange italic.
   - Set-up: "Vets look at two things: where your dog itches, and when." A small drawn calendar and an outline of Miya
     appear side by side.
   - 1/3, the back end: orange circle on the base of the tail, back of the thighs. Miya twists to nibble her rump.
     "If it's mostly the back end, think fleas first. One bite can set off an itch for days in an allergic dog. And
     you might never see the flea." A single flea under a drawn magnifier.
   - 2/3, face, paws, armpits, ears, and worse at certain times of year: circles pop on each; the calendar shades a
     few months. "Face, paws, armpits and ears, worse in some months? That often points to the environment: dust
     mites, pollen, grass. Same as hay fever in people."
   - Teased: "The last one is the sneaky one."
   - 3/3, saved for last: the same face, paws and ears, but the calendar flips page after page and the itch never stops
     (hero beat: calendar pages smear past, impact frame). "Itchy all year round, whatever the season? Food can be the
     reason. And it can look exactly like number two."
   - Dive inside to navy (reuses the B1 cells): the immune system meets a protein it has learned to react to.
     "The only real test is a food trial: one new protein, nothing else, for about eight weeks."
   - Product moment: Allergy & Itch chews tin as a hero object, "Less scratching, more napping." plus a small card,
     "Scratching a lot? Your vet can help you find out which one."
   Ending: "So where does yours scratch? Tell us below." then the logo end card.
   Why it travels: the "where your dog X says a lot" shape the owner picked on night one, and it's practical: every
   owner of an itchy dog has wondered this. Clear story, a twist (food can look like the environment).
   New poses: ★ rump nibble (head turned back to the hip); scratch, sit, lie exist.
   Sources (checked): Vet Times, "Reviewing main varieties of canine allergic skin disease" (FAD: lumbosacral, tail
   base, caudomedial thighs; atopy: face, ears, paws, axillae; food allergy non-seasonal and clinically like atopy);
   dvm360, "The pruritic dog: differential diagnoses"; VCA, elimination-challenge diet trial (at least 8 weeks).

2. Rusty paws: what your dog's paw licking actually means (IDEAS B10) · 40–50 s · accent sky (Allergy & Itch)
   Hook: "See the brown on your dog's paws? Here's why."  ·  Format: myth vs truth, then 3 reasons
   Through-line: the stain is a record of how much they lick, and the licking always has a reason.
   What it covers:
   - Cold open: close-up of Miya's cream front paw with a rusty patch, Miya licking it.
   - Myth: "It's just dirt." Truth: "It's saliva. It carries a natural pigment that stains light fur rusty brown when
     the same spot gets licked again and again."
   - Why so much licking? 1: stress or boredom (Miya staring at a closed door, licking to self-soothe).
   - 2: itch from allergies, environment or food (paw glows sky blue, itch ticks).
   - 3, saved for last: damp fur between the toes lets yeast grow, which itches more, so they lick more: a loop drawn
     as a little circle of arrows. "That's how it snowballs."
   - Product moment: Allergy & Itch chews, "Less licking, more napping." plus "Red, swollen or smelly? See your vet."
   Ending: "Does your dog have rusty paws? Tell us below." then the logo.
   Why it travels: a visible sign every cream or white dog owner has wondered about; Miya's coat shows it perfectly.
   New poses: ★ paw lick (lying, head down to front paw), close-up paw.
   Sources (checked, lighter): AKC, "Why is my dog's fur changing colour" (porphyrins in saliva, Dr Jerry Klein); the
   yeast loop needs a vet-grade source at build (Merck Vet Manual, Malassezia pododermatitis). Lowest confidence here.

3. Your treats are ruining the diet trial (IDEAS B2) · 40–45 s · accent Kangaroo
   Hook: "Your dog's diet trial is failing. Here's why."  ·  Format: timeline (an 8-week calendar)
   Through-line: the immune system needs eight clean weeks; anything with another protein resets the clock.
   What it covers:
   - Cold open: Miya beside her bowl, an 8-week calendar ticking off days.
   - "A food trial means one new protein, and nothing else, for at least eight weeks. That's how long the skin takes
     to calm down."
   - Week 5: a hand drops a chicken treat; the calendar crashes back to day 1 (smear + impact). "One chicken treat
     and the clock starts again."
   - What else counts, each popping onto the calendar: chews, flavoured tablets (some flea and worm chews are
     meat-flavoured), licked plates, the kids' crusts.
   - Saved for last: "So the treats have to be the same single protein."
   - Product moment: Kangaroo treats pouch, "Nothing added. Nothing hidden."
   Ending: "Did you know the treats count? Tell us below." then the logo.
   Why it travels: a direct sequel to the food-allergy film and the "food" twist in ★1; a mistake many owners make.
   New poses: none (sit, pawRaise, wag).
   Sources (checked): VCA, implementing an elimination-challenge diet trial (≥8 weeks, avoid flavoured medications);
   Zoetis owner brochure (anything else restarts the 8 weeks); Olivry et al. 2015, BMC Vet Res 11:225.

4. Why a new food upsets your dog's tummy (IDEAS B8) · 40–50 s · accent sage (Digestive Probiotics)
   Hook: "Changed your dog's food? Here's what happens inside."  ·  Format: timeline (a bowl that fills old → new)
   Through-line: the gut's microbes are tuned to what your dog eats; change it overnight and they can't keep up.
   What it covers:
   - Cold open: Miya sniffing a brand-new bag, tail wagging; hook text.
   - Dive inside to navy/sage: a crowd of little gut microbes, each one used to the old food.
   - Overnight switch: the new food floods in, the microbes panic (smear, chaos), and the poop chart from 14 Oct
     slides in at a 6. "Switch overnight, and you often get soft poop, gas or a picky dog."
   - The slow way: the bowl as a ring, a quarter new, then half, then three quarters, then all of it over about a
     week; the microbes adjust in step. "Mix in a bit more each day, over about a week."
   - Saved for last: "And go even slower if your dog has a sensitive tummy."
   - Product moment: Digestive Probiotics tin, "Firmer poops, happier walks."
   Ending: "Did you switch it slowly last time? Be honest." then the logo.
   Why it travels: practical, every owner changes food at some point; a callback to the poop chart builds a series.
   New poses: sniff (head low; may already exist from earlier builds, else ★).
   Sources (checked): AKC, "The right way to switch dog foods" (5–7 days, start 25 % new; sudden switches can cause
   vomiting, diarrhoea, less appetite).

5. The 10 % treat rule (IDEAS B15) · 30–35 s · accent Rabbit
   Hook: "Here's how many treats your dog should get."  ·  Format: one big visual (a day's food as a bowl, treats as a slice)
   What it covers: the bowl fills for the day → a slice lights up orange, 10 % → three big chews stack up and overflow
   the slice → small single-ingredient bites fit → freeze-dried breaks into pieces, so one treat becomes five.
   Ending: "How many treats did yours get today? Be honest." then the logo.
   Why it travels: a simple rule with a guilty-laugh comment. Less of a story than 1–4.
   New poses: none.
   Sources: WSAVA owner handout "Feeding treats to your dog" (≤10 % of daily calories).
